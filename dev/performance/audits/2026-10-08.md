The release allocation probe produced exactly the same allocation, free, requested-byte, and peak-live counts at every pane count. Every counted allocation was freed during each measured pass in both revisions.

The terminal lifecycle probe retained 39,583,152 requested heap bytes before and after a 10,000-line sustained burst, with **zero allocation calls** during that burst. Narrowing to 80 columns retained 33,400,816 bytes; widening afterward to 320 columns retained 64,538,285 bytes. The production 253×64 pane retained 33,527,661 bytes at 5,000 history rows and 39,587,205 bytes at 5,001. These are allocator-requested bytes, not RSS/PSS.

Replay export generated **21,286,502 bytes** through both collected and spooled paths. The collected allocation probe retained 33,554,432 bytes and peaked at 50,331,860; the spool retained zero and peaked at 262,441 bytes. Both still make roughly 11.56 million temporary allocation calls, so this is a retained-memory advantage rather than allocation-free export.

The separate process probe repeated each mode three times. The `Vec` path increased PSS by **22,112 KiB (21.59 MiB)** in every run; the spool path increased PSS by **0 KiB** in every run. Median export times were 107.388 ms for `Vec` and 111.912 ms for spool. Each baseline and post-export PSS was the median of five samples, 200 ms apart. File-cache residency is not included in process PSS; these figures are not total system-memory consumption.
